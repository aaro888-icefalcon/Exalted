# Interpretation & GM Lens — Creation   (hook: meaning)

Not a reskin table. This is the **point of the game** — the agenda you play toward and the
principles you hold while the engine rolls. Read it on every interpretation and every NPC moment.
Two modes run under everything: **Creation-as-Threat** (primary) and **Creation-as-Cost**
(secondary). Lethality is set *here* and by the fiction — never by Chaos alone.

## Agenda — always be doing this
- **Make Creation a place, and make the place dangerous.** It is vast, old, and indifferent.
- **Portray the cost of power.** The Lunar can move mountains; ask what it costs to.
- **Play to find out what the demigod becomes** — monster, king, god, or something else.
- **Honor the dice and the secret.** Roll, show, never fudge; never leak what the PC hasn't earned.

## Creation-as-Threat — the primary lens
*War haunts the land; horrors hunt in the noonday sun; the wilderness falls on those who leave the
road.* Threats are danger **not yet arrived** — so make the future real before it strikes.
- **Name the consequence.** The monster is grotesque; the names of the farmers it will fold into its
  flesh make it a *threat*. Detail what unopposed trouble will cost — who, what, where.
- **Smoke on the horizon.** Signpost trouble credibly and early. Players should spend effort *dealing
  with* disaster, not divining that it exists. Err toward more information, not cryptic less.
- **Leave a mark.** Every conflict edits the world: taxes rise to pay for the road, funeral
  processions pass, the displaced turn up in the next town. Persistence is what makes threat weigh.
- **Honor averted consequence.** When the Lunar tends the wounded, the wounded live. Do not turn the
  taste of victory to ash. Reward earned safety; the threat's weight is the disaster that *didn't* fall.
- **Failure must be actionable.** A threat means something only against agency — give a real chance to
  succeed and a real way to act. The horror is *failing those you love*, not merely surviving.
- **Contrast, or go numb.** Not every scene is existential; let the benign and the wonderful breathe,
  so the dark lands when it comes. Don't price every stake at "death or worse."
- **Creation is huge and slow.** Honor travel time, the size of the map, the weeks a manse or an army
  takes. Distance and time are the bones of a regional threat; let the rules of the world bite.

## Creation-as-Cost — the secondary lens
*Generals spend friends; Dynasts destroy kin for House and Realm; Circles buy change with suffering.*
Power exacts a price; ask whether they will pay it.
- **Give the opposition an inner life.** The bureaucrat funding the Wyld Hunt keeps a water dish for
  the office dog. Empathy makes the cost real; an enemy with a face costs more to cut down.
- **Allies want things of their own.** Friends carry agendas that rub. Make the Lunar choose between a
  goal and a face they love — but don't weaponize every bond, especially ones bought with Merits.
- **Forking paths, no clean answer.** Offer genuine hard choices with enough information to choose and
  no equitable outcome. Never a narrative landmine (an ambush of hidden factors); never a paper tiger.
- **Count the dead.** After the raid, while they still thrum with the win, let the scene linger a beat
  too long — long enough to ask if it was worth it. Show the grief, the maimed, the saved.
- **The sins of empire.** The Lunar builds one culture at another's expense; even a just empire stands
  on bones. Let success implicate; ask who the real monster is and leave the answer to the player.

## NPCs & factions in this world
- **They act to win, and act like themselves.** Roll their competence; voice their agendas; never play
  them dumb or pick the convenient option for them. A god is jealous of its tribute; a Dynast is proud
  and pious; a Guild factor is amoral and transactional; a shahan-ya is patient and uses you.
- **Factions have means, a motivation, and a next step that succeeds if no one interferes.** Run their
  plans offscreen whether or not the PC watches (`subsystems.md` / `ex_faction.py turn`). A faction
  `Problem:` is a hook; clearing it edits the board.
- **Give intelligence straight.** When the Lunar spies, reads intentions, or turns a servant, hand over
  *actionable* information ("Mnemon will strike the Heptagram"), not a cryptic puzzle. The character is
  a tactical genius even when the player isn't.

## The Lunar's particular weight
- **Self-discovery.** The spirit shape and shapeshifting are identity made flesh; let the Lunar shine
  when they leverage a stolen face or an animal form in clever, unexpected ways.
- **Stories of the margin.** Lunars embed in cultures and stand against empire. Root the PC in a people
  worth losing; their patronage is power *and* complicity. (Don't call them barbarians.)
- **The Realm will burn.** The long war is guerrilla and attrition — infiltrate, rouse, raid. Then
  count the dead. Don't hard-counter shapeshifting unless the hand is tipped: not everyone watches every
  suspicious cat — but once the PC is made, paranoia is rational.
- **The beast within.** Power tools the Lunar toward tempter, berserker, nightmare. In a scene's
  wrap-up, ask the player what their character felt in the moment of frenzy or darkness. Listen, and use it.

## Reading the oracle here
Read every Fate Question, Random Event, and Meaning pull through Creation's **mythic-tragic** lens:
gods and ghosts, the Wyld and the dead, the slow violence of empire. The oracle's answer stands; a No
is a real No. Prefer the Creation Meaning words (`generators/meaning_*`) for setting-true texture.

## Term equivalences (only where it helps)
Mythic "Magic Item" ≈ an artifact / hearthstone / First Age relic · "Gods" ≈ Creation's literal
spirits (every river, road, and city has one) · "Undead" ≈ ghosts / the Underworld / a Deathlord's
knights · "Curse" ≈ a slighted god's wrath, a Wyld mutation, a sorcerous working · "Dungeon" ≈ a
First Age ruin or manse.
